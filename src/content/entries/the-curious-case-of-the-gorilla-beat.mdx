For a long time, there was a drum loop I could recognise almost instantly but could not identify.

<iframe width="560" height="315" src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/EY2yjZfScbU?start=76" title="YouTube video player" frameborder="0" allow="accelerometer; autoplay; clipboard-write; encrypted-media; gyroscope; picture-in-picture; web-share" referrerpolicy="strict-origin-when-cross-origin" allowfullscreen></iframe>

It drives **Death Threat**, from Death in Vegas' 1999 album *The Contino Sessions*. Rather unexpectedly, it also forms the basis of **We Do What The Fuck We Want**, a 1999 live jam by The Prodigy. How was this possible?

The answer eventually arrived in September 2026, by way of an updated sample seeking tool, three additional records, and a musician who replied to my message almost immediately. But it took a few detours to get there.

## An unusually persistent drum loop

I've known both tracks for close to twenty years, and the connection between them has intrigued me for almost as long. Was there some link between Death in Vegas and The Prodigy, or had they simply stumbled upon the same piece of music independently? I was determined to find out.

<iframe width="560" height="315" src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/SgKHUaw5PXw?start=1636" title="YouTube video player" frameborder="0" allow="accelerometer; autoplay; clipboard-write; encrypted-media; gyroscope; picture-in-picture; web-share" referrerpolicy="strict-origin-when-cross-origin" allowfullscreen></iframe>

In **2024**, a member of The Prodigy fan community known as **[No Wuks](https://soundcloud.com/nowuks)** found another appearance of the loop: **Supermarket Of The Damned**, from Jeep Beat Collective's 2001 album *Death Race 2001*.

<iframe width="560" height="315" src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/d81wfRNvVuQ?start=1909" title="YouTube video player" frameborder="0" allow="accelerometer; autoplay; clipboard-write; encrypted-media; gyroscope; picture-in-picture; web-share" referrerpolicy="strict-origin-when-cross-origin" allowfullscreen></iframe>

This was an important clue. Three separate artists had used what appeared to be the same underlying material. Rather than being taken from one another's finished tracks, they might all have drawn on a common source.

I suspected a sample CD from the 1990s. It seemed a reasonable explanation for a loop circulating between otherwise unrelated recordings. I tried isolating it and feeding the result to Shazam, without success.

Meanwhile, The Prodigy fans obtained a rip of **Supermarket Of The Damned (DJ Beats)**, released on vinyl in 2002. It offered a cleaner version of the rhythm, free of some of the distractions present in the full track. A better recording ought to have made identification easier.

It didn't. At least, not yet.

## A useful September update

I had also been testing **[SampleSeek](https://www.sampleseek.co.uk/)**, an audio sample-search tool developed by **Dan Carasco**. Its initial searches did not solve the problem either.

Then, in **September 2026**, Dan introduced an update that allowed SampleSeek to check Google's audio-recognition results as well as Shazam's. I ran the loop through it again.

This time, it returned three additional tracks:

| Year | Artist | Track |
|---|---|---|
| 2011 | Destiny Labs | *No Absolutes?* |
| 2019 | DJ. Mic Check / DJ. Funky C | *Turntablism (Dj'ing)* |
| 2020 | Marc OFX | *Dirty Thoughts* |

Not the original source, but three new paths to investigate. After so many unsuccessful searches, that was quite a result.

![SampleSeek results showing the three additional matches](/images/gorilla-beat/screenshot.jpg)

I first pursued the Turkish turntablism connection suggested by the DJ. Mic Check / DJ. Funky C recording. It seemed possible that the loop had originated on a battle record or another DJ-oriented release. I followed the available leads, but none took me any further.

Then I noticed that **Marc OFX** was active on SoundCloud and Bandcamp. Instead of trying to reconstruct his record collection from a distance, I could ask him directly.

## One message, one answer

I wrote to Marc about the drum loop in *Dirty Thoughts* and asked if he knew where it came from.

He answered almost immediately. Better still, he knew the record.

**It was *Gorilla Beat*, the first track on a Beastie Boys show vinyl prepared for the *Hello Nasty* era.**

After the sample CDs, the isolated audio, the recognition tools and the dead ends, the answer had arrived in a direct message. I was delighted, and a little surprised by the efficiency of the final step.

## A fax from Grand Royal

The record is **Beastie Boys - *Hello Nasty Show Vinyl #5***, catalogue number **BB-SV-5**, a US 12-inch prepared for live DJ use during the 1998 *Hello Nasty* tour. According to the [Beastiemania discography](https://www.beastiemania.com/discog/show.php?r=showvinyl12hn5), it contains instrumentals and short sound snippets. *Gorilla Beat* is the first item on side A.

The packaging is particularly good. A plain white sleeve bears the stamps **JUNE 18 1998** and **BB-SV-5 A/B RECUTS**. The labels are nearly blank, apart from a small **5ARE1** stamp on one side. Attached to the front of the sleeve is a **fax from Grand Royal Records**, dated **June 15, 1998**, listing the contents of the record.

A fax sheet is an agreeable way for a twenty-first-century sample investigation to end.

![Grand Royal fax sheet](/images/gorilla-beat/fax.jpg)

There was also a later route by which this material could have reached collectors. In **2003**, the show-vinyl material was bootlegged as **Beastie Show Breaks**, issued on vinyl and CD. [Beastiemania's April 2003 release notes](https://beastiemania.com/newreleases/release.php?year=2003) document these reissues.

That date matters: Jeep Beat Collective had already released *Supermarket Of The Damned* in **2001**. Whatever copy or recording they used, it could not have been the 2003 bootleg.

## A wrinkle in the dates

Having identified the record, I noticed one further complication.

Death in Vegas used the same loop not only in *Death Threat*, but also in **Song for Penny**, released on the *Lost in Space* soundtrack on **March 31, 1998**. The [soundtrack listing](https://www.soundtrackcollector.com/title/6128/Lost%2BIn%2BSpace) establishes that date, which is nearly three months before the June stamp on the Beastie Boys sleeve.

<iframe width="560" height="315" src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/1Lb3CNe6k4Q?si=ugjy7a4oK0ri6t08&amp;start=1636" title="YouTube video player" frameborder="0" allow="accelerometer; autoplay; clipboard-write; encrypted-media; gyroscope; picture-in-picture; web-share" referrerpolicy="strict-origin-when-cross-origin" allowfullscreen></iframe>

The word **RECUTS** on BB-SV-5 is suggestive, but it is not proof of an earlier pressing. The June date identifies the documented copy; it does not necessarily establish when *Gorilla Beat* was first prepared, or when the loop began circulating. Nor can I say how the Death in Vegas producers obtained it.

For this investigation, though, the central question has an answer. We can name a specific DJ record, a track on it, and the route by which I finally found it. Whether its drum pattern had an earlier life is another question, and not one I intend to answer by guessing.

What I particularly enjoy is that every part of the search helped. No Wuks found the Jeep Beat Collective connection. The fan community supplied a cleaner DJ-beats rip. Dan Carasco's update turned a dead end into three fresh leads. And Marc OFX, who actually knew the record, brought the investigation to a close in a matter of moments.

Thank you to all of them.

I am very pleased to have finally met the gorilla.
