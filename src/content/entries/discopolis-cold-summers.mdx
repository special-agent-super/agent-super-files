I found a Japanese CD by an Edinburgh band called Discopolis. The cover calls it *Cold Summers*. It was released in October 2011, with ten tracks, a rather enthusiastic obi strip, and several pages of Japanese liner notes. So far, so ordinary.

The peculiar part came when I tried to look it up. I could not locate this album in the Rate Your Music or Discogs catalogues, nor could I find the album itself on the major streaming services I checked. Discopolis have not disappeared entirely: several of their singles, and at least one album track on a compilation, can still be found online. But the CD sitting in front of me is far less visible than its contemporary promotional material would lead one to expect.

Rather than leave it there, I have put together a small record of the record: scans of the Japanese packaging, the complete track listing, surviving retailer references, and English translations of the original liner notes and promotional texts. What follows is an archive of the available evidence, not a claim that no other copies or references exist.

**Discopolis - Cold Summers**, Tower Records limited-price edition, Japan, 2011.

The title discrepancy is worth retaining. The packaging shown here identifies the record as *Cold Summers*, while [Tower Records' surviving product page](https://tower.jp/item/2940052) lists it under the artist's name, *Discopolis*. Searching for only one title could therefore miss the other.

## Another Japanese edition

There is a further complication. [Tower Records also lists a standard edition](https://tower.jp/item/2931145), catalogue **RYECD-110**, with a different barcode (**4526180048886**) and a **different ten-track programme**. The Japanese second-hand retailer [Suruga-ya](https://www.suruga-ya.jp/product/detail/221128349) independently reproduces the standard-edition listing. Both editions carry the same release date, 5 October 2011.

| No. | Tower limited-price CD (RYECD-110TW) | Standard CD (RYECD-110) |
|---|---|---|
| 01 | Cold Summers | Summer Nightmares |
| 02 | Bears Kill Kids | We Should Have Been Born In Canada |
| 03 | Summer Nightmares | Bears Kill Kids |
| 04 | Weekend Burnings | Lofty Ambitions |
| 05 | Zenithobia | Cold Summers turn to Scottish Winters |
| 06 | Lofty Ambitions | When Ghosts Get Angry |
| 07 | Timber Merchants | Zenithobia Final |
| 08 | We Should Have Been Born In Canada | Weekend Burnings |
| 09 | When Ghosts Get Angry | Lofty Ambitions (One Dollar Dave & Megademandeman Remix) |
| 10 | Lofty Ambitions (One Dollar Dave & Megadamandaman Remix) | Summer Nightmares (One Day Diary Remix) |

This is more than a change of running order. *Timber Merchants* appears only in the limited-price listing; *Cold Summers turn to Scottish Winters* and *Summer Nightmares (One Day Diary Remix)* appear only in the standard one. *Zenithobia* and *Zenithobia Final* may be different versions, although the titles alone cannot establish that. I have not inspected a physical copy of the standard edition, so this comparison documents **what the retailer listings say**, not a verified comparison of the two discs' audio.

## What became of the album?

As of October 2026, I have not been able to establish a Discogs or Rate Your Music entry for these Japanese CD editions, or an album-level listing on the main streaming platforms I searched. That is a statement about the search, not proof of universal non-existence. In fact, [Spotify](https://open.spotify.com/artist/7vfpOh4NjK9TO3n70v1G1i) still lists Discopolis singles such as *Lofty Ambitions* and *Zenithobia*, and [Apple Music](https://music.apple.com/us/artist/discopolis/451461373) has a selection of their later releases. The song *Summer Nightmares* also appears on the [2012 *Lip Service* soundtrack](https://music.apple.com/us/album/summer-nightmares/523679376?i=523679385).

Meanwhile, the surviving Japanese product descriptions are full of confidence. They describe a trio of nineteen-year-olds, a T in the Park appearance after just two gigs, enthusiastic coverage by *NME* and *The Scotsman*, and heavy BBC Radio 1 airplay. These were the claims being made **to sell the album in 2011**; where they have not been independently checked, I treat them as contemporary promotional evidence rather than settled biography.

The gap between that moment of apparent momentum and the album's present obscurity is what makes this object worth keeping. Below are the contemporary texts, translated into English and kept separate from my own notes.

## Archival document 1: Japanese obi strip

**Original:** Promotional copy printed on the obi of RYECD-110TW.
**Translation:** Japanese to English.

The up-and-coming trio of nineteen-year-olds, consisting of Fergus Cook, Laurie Corlett Donald, and Dave Lloyd. The three were classmates in high school and formed the band last year. After just two live performances, they achieved the extraordinary feat of appearing at the prestigious UK festival *T in the Park 2011*.

Their performance was highly praised by music magazines, and they subsequently continued to play energetically at the *Reading/Leeds* festivals and local shows. They also received high praise from *NME*, which described them as "intense Friendly Fires, or Deadmau5 destroying a school hall."

Despite not having released a single official record, their music was heavily played on radio stations, including BBC Radio 1, and became a subject of considerable attention.

Furthermore, their debut single, "Lofty Ambitions," released in August, is already being praised by the press as "a record so wonderfully beautiful that it takes only one listen to realise it. Flamboyant synthesisers, dreamy pop vocals, astonishing guitar riffs, and pounding kick drums... It's all there."

The debut album from a much-discussed band whose breakthrough seems certain, receiving its first release anywhere in the world exclusively through Rallye.

**Obi details:** Rallye Label / RYECD110TW; ¥1,980 (¥1,886 excluding tax); Tower Records limited price; commentary by Mai Hatori (*rockin'on*); lyrics and Japanese translations included; world-first release. The small-print copyright notice prohibits unauthorised duplication, commercial exploitation and making the recorded audio available for online transmission without permission.

## Archival document 2: Japanese liner notes

**Original:** Mai Hatori (*rockin'on*), September 2011. Printed inside the Japanese CD.
**Translation:** Japanese to English; preserves the original's critical tone and sequence of paragraphs. Personal and promotional claims remain attributed to the source.

With chillwave now firmly established in the indie scene, the boundary between dance acts and rock bands has become even more ambiguous than before. The so-called crossover sound of dance and rock has also undergone considerable changes in the role it plays. As the phrase "the internet age" has become so commonplace that hardly anyone even bothers to use it anymore, the spread of the internet and the emergence of lo-fi as a new popular aesthetic have made the world of music completely seamless. It no longer seems strange to encounter any kind of sound anywhere. Washed Out, the American act from Georgia that has been steadily gaining fans here in Japan, is perhaps the greatest example of this development.

Amid these circumstances, Discopolis, a new trio based in Edinburgh, produces a rare sound combining the hedonism of dance music, the dynamism of electronic rock bands, and the narrative quality of bedroom electropop.

Discopolis consists of Fergus Cook (synths/vocals/guitar), Laurie Corlett Donald (synths/guitar/live sampling), and Dave Lloyd (synths/drums/engineer). The three are friends from their high school days, and the band is a trio of just nineteen-year-olds. More on their background later, but their soulful sound and exquisitely stylish appearance are reminiscent of Friendly Fires. (Incidentally, the British music magazine *NME* has described them as "intense Friendly Fires, or Deadmau5 destroying a school hall.")

"We formed Discopolis while experimenting with different projects. It's been about a year since we got together, so we're still going through the growing pains of adolescence. Right now we're just doing our best not to let our fans down. (As for the origin of the band's name, it's nothing to do with the image of the Bee Gees or anything like that, dancing around wearing gold necklaces. We took it from Lifelike and Kris Menace.)"

What can be gathered from the band's own account is that Discopolis is not just another band aiming to make music people can dance to. Kris Menace, who emerged from Germany and has demonstrated his many talents as a producer, remixer, and DJ, is known among rock fans and indie listeners for his remix work with LCD Soundsystem and Röyksopp, among others. He was one of the figures who supported the flourishing electronic music scene of the 2000s from behind the scenes. His debut single, "Discopolis," featuring the French house pioneer Lifelike, is the source of their name.

Indeed, I think this alone makes it clear that Discopolis approaches music with a different mindset from the flood of lo-fi DIY acts and pop/rock bands whose primary aim is to make people dance.

Here, I would like to briefly trace the band's history. As the earlier quote suggests, the band was formed only about a year ago. According to what I have heard, Fergus and Laurie, who had attended the same school but had never spoken to each other, happened to meet at T in the Park in 2007 and immediately hit it off. They began making music together in a four-piece guitar band called Ryan's Mothership. Around that time, Dave was working as a DJ at clubs in Edinburgh.

The three only began writing music together and performing live earlier this year. After just two live performances, they suddenly found themselves appearing at Scotland's T in the Park festival and Britain's famous Reading/Leeds Festival. Naturally, the band was almost completely unknown before these festival appearances, but performing there instantly captured the attention of local listeners and the media.

Then, in August, they released "Lofty Ambitions," a track that had already become a talking point online, as a single through the new Scottish digital label Eli & Oz. (Incidentally, Eli & Oz uploads five tracks every month to the online music service SoundCloud, making its carefully selected releases available there before beginning promotional activities. It is a unique label that seems to have brought Scotland's DIY ethos up to date, adopting a revolutionary approach that draws a clear distinction from conventional record labels.)

And now comes *Cold Summers*, their debut album, receiving its first release anywhere in the world exclusively in Japan.

While this background is certainly interesting, a wide variety of inspirations can also be detected in Discopolis's musical world. Fergus, who handles the lyrics, says that the album is filled with the experiences of their lives growing up in Edinburgh and the feelings of "angry teenagers." My first impression upon listening to the album was that it is, above all, emotional and fresh.

"When you list the sounds that have influenced you, I think your perspective becomes narrower, or rather, you start developing fixed ideas about the band, which isn't really desirable. But if I had to name my top three, they would be M83, Deadmau5, and Boards of Canada. We are also influenced by the home recordings of Modest Mouse, and by Martyn Bennett, who combined techno and bagpipes."

As the band itself acknowledges, a variety of references can be found hidden within the sound of Discopolis. These include the influences that shaped the band in the first place, but they extend far beyond the synth-pop, house, techno, soul music, and Daft Punk of the 1980s, or the French electro scene led by the likes of Justice and the post-electro, new-generation bedroom pop associated with the Avalanches.

The album is also permeated by a remarkable diversity of musical influences: nostalgic, lyrical melodies with a distinctly Scottish character; the melancholy of 2000s UK rock associated with the so-called "new emotional" movement; and the grand, post-rock approach. Yet none of these elements becomes scattered or disconnected. Instead, they come together as cinematic pop music, and therein lies the uniqueness of Discopolis.

Since the beginning of the 2000s, from Franz Ferdinand to Animal Collective, the pop music that indie bands aspire to create has become increasingly permeated by so-called "pop art," something quite different from what came before. Nevertheless, as with any movement, the term "pop music" itself has become worn out through overuse. There is even a tendency for music that originates from indie bands to revert instantly to easily forgotten three-minute pop songs once its indie identity has been stripped away.

Against this background, I feel that the pop music Discopolis aims for is not the kind with a short shelf life. It is universal pop music that values an atmospheric sensibility. Perhaps this is something that could only have emerged from the turbulent adolescence they spent in Edinburgh, a city that, while compact, encompasses both the melancholy of its old town and its suburbs.

In any case, I suspect it will not be long before this sound, capable of stirring the emotions of listeners equally on a dancefloor, in the corner of a room, or on a large stage, is heard around the world. Here is the arrival of a band that vividly marks a new chapter in the ongoing dialogue between pop and dance music.

*September 2011 - Mai Hatori (rockin'on)*

A sound destined for a breakthrough, miraculously combining the dreamy vocals of Kyte and M83, the romantic dance beats of Friendly Fires, and the flamboyant synthesizers of the Ed Banger artists!

Formed just one year ago (!), with all three members only nineteen years old (!), the band made it to T in the Park, one of the UK's largest and most prestigious festivals, after playing just two live shows. They received rave reviews from publications including *NME* and *The Scotsman*.

### Discopolis - artist biography (HMV promotional text)

Discopolis is a trio of just nineteen-year-olds from Edinburgh, Scotland, consisting of Fergus Cook (synths/vocals/guitar), Laurie Corlett Donald (synths/guitar/live sampling), and Dave Lloyd (synths/drum effects/engineer).

The three, who were classmates in high school, formed the band last year and began performing live this year. After just two live performances, they achieved the extraordinary feat of appearing at the prestigious Scottish festival T in the Park 2011 (whose lineup also included Coldplay, Arctic Monkeys, Foo Fighters, Beady Eye, The Strokes, Weezer, Primal Scream, and others).

Their performance received rave reviews from the local music press, with one publication declaring, "They will soon be playing much bigger venues." They subsequently continued to perform energetically at the Reading/Leeds festivals and local shows, earning high praise from *NME*, which described them as "intense Friendly Fires, or Deadmau5 destroying a school hall."

Despite not having a single official release, their songs attracted attention through heavy airplay on radio stations including BBC Radio 1 and DAB.

Their debut single, "Lofty Ambitions," scheduled for release in August, had already received enthusiastic praise from the press:

> "Even after just a brief listen, you can tell that this is a wonderfully beautiful record. Flamboyant synths, dreamy pop vocals, astonishing guitar riffs, and pounding kick drums... It's all there."

The quotation is attributed in the Japanese original to **QUALITY EMERGING MUSIC**.

The debut album from a much-talked-about band widely expected to break through, receiving its world-first release exclusively through Rallye!
